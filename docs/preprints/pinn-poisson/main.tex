\section{Introduction}
A \textbf{Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN)} solving the \textbf{2D Poisson equation} \texttt{$\Delta$u = f} on the unit square \texttt{$\Omega$ = [0,1]\textsuperscript{2}} with Dirichlet boundary data \texttt{u = g} on \texttt{$\partial$$\Omega$}, on \textbf{Stack E (Warp substrate) + PyTorch} (single-stack). Soft-constraint Raissi-2019 formulation. Verified \textbf{two-pronged}: vs \textbf{analytic} Poisson solutions (golden values, $\geq$3 independent-reference anchors incl. one inhomogeneous \texttt{f$\neq$0} MMS case) AND vs a \textbf{classical finite-difference (FD) reference} (a reusable testkit surface), plus \textbf{solution-verification convergence with collocation density}.

\textbf{CPU-only scope (scope honesty).} This environment has \textbf{no CUDA driver}, so the "Stack E" sim runs \textbf{entirely on CPU} (Warp-CPU + PyTorch-CPU). The GPU-Warp path is \textbf{unexercised} here. "Stack E: PASS" therefore means the CPU substrate + torch$\leftrightarrow$wp interop + capture bridge are verified --- it is \textbf{not} a claim that GPU Warp is verified (same scope-honesty discipline as neural-ca's "WGSL-shader-verified-but-browser-deploy-unexercised" note).

\section{Method}
A fully-connected \texttt{tanh} MLP \texttt{N\_$\theta$: (x, y) $\mapsto$ u} is trained by minimizing a composite soft-constraint loss over sampled points:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}\noindent
L($\theta$) = L\_interior($\theta$) + $\lambda$ $\cdot$ L\_boundary($\theta$) \\
L\_interior = mean over interior collocation pts of ( $\Delta$u\_$\theta$ $-$ f )\textsuperscript{2} \\
L\_boundary = mean over $\partial$$\Omega$ pts of ( u\_$\theta$ $-$ g )\textsuperscript{2}
\end{quote}

where \texttt{$\Delta$u\_$\theta$ = u\_xx + u\_yy} is formed by \textbf{second-order \texttt{torch.autograd.grad}} (the network output differentiated twice w.r.t. its inputs). Optimizer: Adam, seeded (\texttt{config.seed}, default 42). The trained network is then \textbf{frozen} and evaluated on the eval grid; the frozen-network field is the verified artifact.

\section{Evaluation}
\textbf{Prong 1 --- vs analytic (golden, \texttt{analytical\_l2 = 1e-3}).} The frozen-network field matches each analytic anchor within relative discrete-L2 \texttt{1e-3}. This is the load-bearing acceptance gate. $\geq$3 independent-reference anchors per golden table (Cat-3 HARD\_FAILs otherwise, spec \S{} 2.4).

\textbf{Prong 2 --- vs classical FD (\texttt{fd\_l2 = 1e-2}).} The frozen-network field matches the pure-NumPy 5-point-Laplacian FD solution of the same BVP within relative discrete-L2 \texttt{1e-2}. \textbf{The FD solver is a high-precision NUMERICAL baseline anchored to the analytic set --- it is NOT an independent reference} (it inherits its own \texttt{O(h\textsuperscript{2})} discretization error). \texttt{fd\_l2} is intentionally \textbf{wider} than \texttt{analytical\_l2} for exactly this reason.

\textbf{FD-reference rigor (mutation substitute).} The FD reference's mutation target is DEFERRED to task-9 (D-MUTATION, rule-of-three --- first classical-reference). Its correctness rests instead on: (a) point-match vs all three analytic anchors, AND (b) a \textbf{convergence-order check} --- refining \texttt{h $\in$ \{1/16,1/32,1/64,1/128\}} against the MMS analytic solution, the observed discrete-L2 order is \textbf{$\approx$ 2} (\texttt{O(h\textsuperscript{2})}, 5-point Laplacian truncation). A real solver bug breaks the \emph{order}, not just a tolerance --- so this MMS-grade order check is the rigor substitute for the deferred mutation testing (Phase-0 \texttt{mms/} infrastructure is the structural precedent).

\textbf{Prong 3 --- convergence with collocation density.} Spec \S{} 5.12: the PINN's error vs the analytic solution decreases as the interior collocation-point count grows (envelope-scoped to the trained domain).

\textbf{Declared PBT invariants (gate-11, $\geq$2; impl \texttt{tools/testkit/property/sims/pinn-poisson/}):}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{boundary\_residual\_bounded}} --- for boundary points sampled within \texttt{$\partial$$\Omega$}, \texttt{|u\_$\theta$(x,y) $-$ g(x,y)|} is bounded by a small envelope (the trained boundary-loss scale). Envelope-scoped: re-declare the regime on falsification, do NOT widen (PINNs do not extrapolate; neural-ca \texttt{field\_values\_bounded} precedent).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{pde\_residual\_bounded}} --- for interior points sampled within \texttt{$\Omega$}, \texttt{|$\Delta$u\_$\theta$(x,y) $-$ f(x,y)|} is bounded by a small envelope (the trained interior-loss scale). Envelope-scoped identically.
\end{enumerate}

Both sample \textbf{within the trained domain} (the envelope is the training regime, not an extrapolation claim). The bounds are MEASURED + locked at Stage 1b-PINN.
