--- Why numba didn't deliver the expected speedup ---

R18 routing assumed numba would compile the inner per-pair math to
near-native loops. R19 surface confirmed numba IS producing a 2.8x
speedup on the per-pair math (5s/step → 0.5-1s/step). But the
per-step total is still 7-10s because:

  1. cKDTree.query_pairs at N=1M: ~3-5 s/step. C-implemented but the
     algorithmic cost (O(N log N) tree build + O(N × neighbors)
     query) plus the ~50M-entry symmetrize/lexsort is genuinely 3-5 s.

  2. Pair-array materialization (np.concatenate of two 25M-element
     arrays + np.lexsort over 50M elements): ~1-2 s/step. Vectorized
     but unavoidable at this M.

  3. Numba inner math: ~0.5-1 s/step. The speedup is real but only
     ~10-20% of the per-step total.

  4. _trajectory_to_step_states post-loop (11 frames × cKDTree +
     density_jit + StepState builder): ~20-40 s total at canonical
     scale (each frame requires a fresh cKDTree).

  5. h5py write of ~600 MB H5 file: ~30-60 s.

The dominant cost is items 1+2 (cKDTree orchestration + pair-array
materialization), which numba does not accelerate. The R19 surface
analysis identified this correctly ("cKDTree itself is the per-step
floor") but underestimated the magnitude — projecting 3-4 s/step
when actual is 4.5-7 s.

--- Total wall-clock decomposition (revised) ---

  Per-step (1000 steps):
    cKDTree.query_pairs ............ ~3-5 s
    np.concatenate + np.lexsort .... ~1-2 s
    density_evolution_jit .......... ~0.5-1 s
    velocity/position integrate .... ~0.05 s
    ─────────────────────────────
    Total per step .................. ~5-8 s

  Per-frame (11 frames, post-loop):

    density_jit .................... ~0.5-1 s

    ─────────────────────────────
    Total per frame ................. ~7-11 s × 11 = 77-121 s

  H5 write:                           ~30-60 s

  Grand total: 5000-8000 s + 77-121 s + 30-60 s = ~85-135 min

Hmm — that's actually within the R19 projection band. But observed
202 min suggests per-step is closer to 12 s, or the 11-frame post-
loop is closer to 5-10 min, or something else is amplifying.

Possible amplifiers not in the analysis:
  - L3 cache pressure: 50M-pair arrays (~1.2 GB) plus position/
    velocity arrays (~48 MB) plus cKDTree internal state (~80 MB)
    blow past L3 (usually 16-30 MB on this class of CPU). Each
    step's vectorized numpy ops + numba inner has to re-load from
    DRAM. DRAM bandwidth ~40 GB/s on this hardware; 1.2 GB array
    × multiple sweeps per step = serialized DRAM reads of ~5 GB =
    ~125 ms just for memory traffic.
  - Numba JIT caching: first-call compilation cost (~5-30 s); subsequent
    calls amortize. Probably not the issue since we observed multi-
    minute steady-state, not one big startup spike.
  - Allocator pressure: Python/numpy GC behavior on the ~50M-entry
    arrays per step. Could add a few hundred ms per step at this scale.

Net: per-step is probably 7-12 s rather than 4-5 s. 1000 steps =
117-200 min. Plus post-loop = 120-210 min. We observed 202 min and
were still going, consistent with the upper end of this range.
