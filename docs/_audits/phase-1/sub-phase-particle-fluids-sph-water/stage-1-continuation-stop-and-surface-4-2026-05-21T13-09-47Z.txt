--- Why the speedup was less than projected ---

The R18 routing analysis assumed numba would compile the per-pair
inner math to a near-native loop with negligible overhead. The
observed 2.8× speedup vs the pure-NumPy vectorized path suggests:

  1. The pure-NumPy variant was ALREADY using SIMD-vectorized
     intrinsics (AVX2/AVX-512) for the per-pair arithmetic. The
     gain from numba is "remove intermediate (M, 3) array
     allocations" + "lower interpreter dispatch" — but NOT "convert
     interpreted Python to compiled C", because the bottleneck
     wasn't interpreted Python at the inner-math level.
  2. The per-step cost is dominated NOT by the per-pair math but by
     the cKDTree.query_pairs call + lexsort + symmetrize, which
     numba does NOT accelerate (those run inside scipy/numpy C).
  3. Memory-bandwidth-bound work for moving ~50M pairs of (i, j)
     indices + ~25M unique (rx, ry, rz, v_x, v_y, v_z) bytes through
     L3 cache per step. Numba can't speed this up.

Per-step breakdown estimate (78 min / 1000 steps × 60 s/min):
   ~4.7 s/step
     ├─ pair_lists_from_positions (cKDTree + lexsort): ~3-4 s
     │  (dominates; not numba-accelerated)
     ├─ density_evolution_jit (numba inner): ~0.5-1 s (was ~5s in
     │  pure-NumPy variant; numba speedup is in here)
     ├─ velocity/position integrate + frame capture cadence: ~0.5 s

The R18 optimism was concentrated on the wrong bottleneck. cKDTree
itself is the per-step floor.
