## 1. Purpose

Bring the verified Stam-Fedkiw smoke solver to the site as an interactive, visually-stunning WebGPU instrument that **proves it solves the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations** — not merely looks like smoke. The moat versus every pretty-but-uncertified web fluid toy (PavelDoGreat and its many forks; matsuoka-601's MLS-MPM oceans) is the PROVE layer: a live **divergence-residual heatmap**, a **checkerboard null-space probe** (§ 4.3 — the textbook collocated-grid failure made visceral in one click), an **analytic Taylor–Green decay overlay** (measured kinetic energy against the closed-form Navier–Stokes solution, live), a **run-twice determinism hash**, a **live gate re-run against the committed f64 canonical on the visitor's GPU**, and **live A/B toggles that let the visitor *watch the numerics improve*** (semi-Lagrangian→MacCormack pulling the measured decay curve toward the analytic one; collocated→staggered dropping the divergence residual, v2). The finger-painting smoke the visitor plays with is driven by *the exact solver that reproduces our verified reference*.

## 2. Why this sim (and positioning)

- **FACT.** The physics is already verified: the NumPy reference passes MMS order-of-accuracy (gate 5 — two tests in `packages/eulerian-smoke/tests/test_mms_convergence.py`: MacCormack advection observed p≈2 on the N∈{32,64,128} ladder with dt∝dx², and projection Helmholtz-decomposition p≈2), the `divergence_free_post_projection` and `smoke_density_nonneg` PBT invariants (gate 12, `packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/invariants.py`), and a `bit-exact-same-hw` determinism gate (`packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/sim.py`). The web work is *port + productionize + make-visible*, not *invent-and-verify*.
- **FACT.** A committed canonical capture already exists (2D `lid-driven-cavity-128sq-re100-seed42-step1000`, 128², f64, sha-pinned, fields u/v/density at 11 checkpoints) — so the browser build can bind to a **real `capture_roundtrip` gate from day one** (the rd2d pattern), never needing the `boids-2d` "experimental — not yet gated" label.
- **FACT (the honest weakness is a feature — two-layer version).** The textbook criticism of collocated central differencing is the **checkerboard null-space**: the stencil `(q[i+1] − q[i−1])/2dx` never reads `q[i]`, so the oscillating mode `q[i] = (−1)^i` registers as having *zero* derivative — divergence the projection is structurally blind to (Bridson & Müller, *Fluid Simulation* SIGGRAPH course notes, ch. 2, crediting Harlow & Welch 1965 for the MAC-staggered fix). Our reference documents the operational consequence in its own words: the composed `∇·∇p` is the "wide" Laplacian `(p[i+2] − 2p[i] + p[i−2])/4dx²`, NOT the 5-point Laplacian the Jacobi sweep solves — the classical Stam-on-collocated inconsistency that leaves an **O(dx²) residual divergence even at convergence** (`packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/reference/stable_fluids.py:219` docstring; the `_DIV_TOL = 1e-1` empirical floor in `packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/invariants.py:37`). This demo turns both layers into instruments: the residual heatmap shows the floor; the null-space probe shows *why* it exists; the staggered A/B (v2) shows the fix.
- **FACT (the canonical's name is itself an honesty lesson).** The "lid-driven-cavity" capture is a periodic shear-layer IC approximation of a cavity, self-documented as such in the reference (`packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/sim.py:333-361`). The demo says so out loud (§ 4.2) — and gets a free narrative gift: a tanh shear layer in a periodic box is the classic **Kelvin–Helmholtz instability** setup, so the gated canonical doubles as a beautiful named-instability preset (§ 4.1).
- **INFERENCE.** Smoke is the single best effort-to-wow ratio of the built fluids — the sim runs cheaply at 256² and the aesthetic ceiling (PavelDoGreat) is proven-achievable in a browser.

### 2.2 Prior art & positioning (web + literature survey, 2026-07-03; re-verified against primary sources for v0.2)

**The solver lineage (what we port):**
- **Stam 1999**, "Stable Fluids" (SIGGRAPH '99, [DOI 10.1145/311535.311548](https://doi.org/10.1145/311535.311548)) — unconditionally-stable advection by the *method of characteristics* (the term "semi-Lagrangian" was attached to it later, e.g. by Selle 2008) + Helmholtz–Hodge/Chorin projection. The reference `stable_fluids.py` implements this.
- **Stam 2003**, "Real-Time Fluid Dynamics for Games" ([GDC PDF](http://graphics.cs.cmu.edu/nsp/course/15-464/Fall09/papers/StamFluidforGames.pdf)) — the practical collocated recipe; hard-codes `for (k=0; k<20; k++)` Gauss-Seidel in both `diffuse()` and `project()`; states cell-centered storage explicitly.
- **Fedkiw, Stam, Jensen 2001**, "Visual Simulation of Smoke" ([DOI 10.1145/383259.383260](https://doi.org/10.1145/383259.383260)) — brings **Steinhoff's vorticity confinement** (invented for helicopter CFD) into graphics, and explicitly credits its **staggered-grid** arrangement for "less artificial dissipation" vs Stam's voxel centers.
- **Harris, GPU Gems Ch. 38** ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-vi-beyond-triangles/chapter-38-fast-fluid-dynamics-simulation-gpu)) — the canonical GPU port; chooses **Jacobi** for "simplicity and easy implementation," typically 40–80 iterations, and warns "it is not a good idea to go below 20 iterations, because the error is noticeable."
- **Crane, Llamas, Tariq, GPU Gems 3 Ch. 30** ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-v-physics-simulation/chapter-30-real-time-simulation-and-rendering-3d-fluids)) — 3D GPU fluids, cell-centered "for pedagogical simplicity" with the staggered caveat; implements the **clamp-variant** MacCormack limiter.

**Accuracy upgrades (verified findings — see § 3):**
- **Selle, Fedkiw, Kim, Liu, Rossignac 2008**, "An Unconditionally Stable MacCormack Method" (J. Sci. Comput. 35:350–371, [DOI 10.1007/s10915-007-9166-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10915-007-9166-4)) — 2nd-order in space *and* time with **two** SL advections (BFECC needs three); describes **two limiters** (min/max clamp, and revert-to-first-order on new extrema — the authors prefer reversion); measured temporal orders 2.1–2.3 vs 1.0 for the first-order baseline.
- **Zhang, Bridson, Greif 2015**, IVOCK (SIGGRAPH 2015, [DOI 10.1145/2766982](https://doi.org/10.1145/2766982)) — 10–25% extra runtime; "the divergence of the curl of the correcting streamfunction is identically zero, hence the right-hand-side of the pressure projection is unchanged."
- **Zehnder, Narain, Thomaszewski 2018**, advection-reflection (SIGGRAPH 2018, [DOI 10.1145/3197517.3201324](https://doi.org/10.1145/3197517.3201324)) — `R = 2P − I` applied at the half step; abstract claims **two orders of magnitude reduction in energy loss**. Corrected cost accounting: **two advections AND two projections per step** ("effectively identical computational cost to an advection-projection solver with half the time step"), not merely 2× projection.
- **Gagniere, Hyde, Marquez-Razon, Jiang, Teran 2020** ([arXiv:2003.12227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.12227)) — the log-log slope evidence, **correctly attributed (v0.2 fix):** their Fig. 16 measures slope ≈1 for explicit semi-Lagrangian and slope ≈2 for *their BSLQB scheme* — they never test MacCormack. The MacCormack≈2nd-order empirical evidence is **Selle 2008** (and our own committed gate-5 MMS test, which is the citation the demo actually displays: observed p≈2 on our ladder).
- **McAdams, Sifakis, Teran 2010**, MGPCG ([PDF](https://math.ucdavis.edu/~jteran/papers/MST10.pdf)) — grid-size-independent convergence, ~9–13 iterations to a 1e-4 residual reduction across 64³–512³; the escalation path for 512²/3D (harder on WebGPU).
- **Grid-criticism source of record (v0.2 addition):** Bridson & Müller, *Fluid Simulation* course notes ([UBC PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/fluidsimulation/fluids_notes.pdf)) — the null-space statement quoted in § 2, the authoritative framing for our EXPLAIN panel (both stencils are O(dx²)-*accurate*; the collocated one has the wrong *null-space*).

**Reference code to mine (v0.2: descriptions corrected against the actual repos):**
- **[PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation)** — the aesthetic gold standard. Verified defaults: 128² sim / **1024² dye**, `CURL: 30`, `PRESSURE_ITERATIONS: 20`, separate density/velocity dissipation (1.0 / 0.2), bloom (8 iters, threshold 0.6, soft-knee 0.7, res 256), sunrays (res 196), splat force 6000 / radius 0.25. **Two visually load-bearing techniques the v0.1 survey missed:** (1) `SHADING` — a pseudo-3D normal built from four dye-neighbor samples, lighting the dye like a relief surface (a large fraction of the look); (2) a blue-noise **dithering texture** on the display pass (kills banding in dark bloom gradients). **And one numerics correction:** Pavel does NOT run Jacobi from zero — the previous frame's pressure is decayed by `PRESSURE: 0.8` and used as **warm start**, which is *why* 20 iterations look acceptable. Our gated canonical is zero-initialized (matching the reference); the warm-start toggle (§ 3.2) therefore has real prior-art provenance and a visible effect.
- **[matsuoka-601/WebGPU-Ocean](https://github.com/matsuoka-601/webgpu-ocean)** (v0.2 addition) — the current WebGPU-fluid headline (530★): MLS-MPM in WGSL compute, ~100k–300k particles, screen-space fluid rendering, fixed-point `atomicAdd` grid scatter. Its README candidly notes "occasionally the simulation explodes" — zero correctness instrumentation. Prime positioning evidence.
- **[kishimisu/WebGPU-Fluid-Simulation](https://github.com/kishimisu/WebGPU-Fluid-Simulation)** — Stam 2003 in WGSL compute with buffer-based ping-pong; an early-WebGPU learning project (10 commits), useful as a WGSL port sketch, not a production reference.
- **[jeantimex/fluid](https://github.com/jeantimex/fluid)** — corrected description (v0.2): a WebGPU **SPH + PIC/FLIP particle** fluid (not a grid solver) with GPU prefix-sum sorting and four renderers — prior art for the *SPH* web port, cross-referenced here for the render pipeline only. **[jeantimex/procedural-clouds](https://github.com/jeantimex/procedural-clouds)** — confirmed: compute-populated ping-ponged 3D density texture + full-screen ray-march (the optional-3D path, § 5).
- **Negative space (v0.2 addition):** the official [webgpu-samples](https://github.com/webgpu/webgpu-samples) repo has **no fluid sample at all** (verified against its full sample directory) — even the canonical showcase lacks one.

**White space (positioning).** Every surveyed web fluid demo is an *aesthetic* artifact that makes **no correctness claim** — verified across PavelDoGreat, matsuoka-601's three sims, kishimisu, and jeantimex: no divergence readouts, no convergence plots, no verification gates, and in the most-starred case an explicit "sometimes it explodes." The defensible novelty is the **intersection of PavelDoGreat-class visuals with a live, honest verification instrument** — the project's moat, uncontested on the open web.

## 3. Algorithm & accuracy architecture

**The load-bearing decision: the gated canonical path is the faithful collocated port; the upgrades are labeled exploratory toggles.** The `capture_roundtrip` gate compares against the collocated f64 canonical, so the *gated* solver must be the collocated reference exactly. Changing the numerics would deviate from the canonical — so MAC-staggered / IVOCK / reflection / the MacCormack limiter ship as **clearly-labeled "exploratory — not the gated canonical" toggles**, forced OFF during any canonical/capture/gate run. This preserves the gate *and* showcases the frontier (§ 4.3 A/B). Promoting an upgrade to canonical is a backend follow-up (a new Python reference + new capture + `new_canonical` gate — exactly what the `eulerian-smoke-frontier-*` packages are for).

### 3.1 Gated canonical core (must match the reference bit-for-bit modulo f32)

Operator order per step, matching `stable_fluids_step` + the 2D canonical driver: **advect velocity → (source, zero in canonical) → diffuse → project → advect density.**

- **Velocity advection:** MacCormack (predictor + backward corrector, **no limiter** — the omission is deliberate and documented: the reference docstring at `packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/reference/stable_fluids.py:177` explains clamping would mute the 2nd-order convergence the gate-5 OOA test certifies). Backtrace with bilinear interpolation, explicit lexicographic vertex ordering (f00, f01, f10, f11 — determinism charter clause 2).
- **Density advection (v0.2 correction):** **plain bilinear semi-Lagrangian, NOT MacCormack**, applied *after* projection (`packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/sim.py:450-454`). The asymmetry is part of the canonical and must be ported as-is. Density **is a gated captured field** (u, v, density — pressure is not captured).
- **Boundary conditions:** fully periodic via wrapped indexing (the reference's `np.mod`/`np.roll`; no ghost cells). **The reference has no wall BCs of any kind** — the drawable-obstacle feature (§ 4.1) is therefore web-only exploratory numerics, not a port.
- **Diffusion:** explicit 5-point Laplacian, single step (`ν·dt·∇²`), `packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/reference/stable_fluids.py:337-339`.
- **Projection:** **Jacobi, fixed 20 iterations, pressure zero-initialized every step** (v0.2: confirmed — no warm start in the reference), collocated centered-difference divergence (`:219`) and gradient (`:292`), Jacobi sweep at `:283` (all in `packages/eulerian-smoke/eulerian_smoke/reference/stable_fluids.py`). Jacobi (not Gauss-Seidel) because parallel cells cannot read updated neighbors in-place — the standard GPU substitution (Harris GPU Gems 38; Stam's 20 GS iters → 20 Jacobi).
- **No vorticity confinement (v0.2 correction).** The 2D reference has none; the 3D solver's Fedkiw-confinement runs at ε=0.0 in the 3D canonical. Any 2D confinement in the demo is a **new aesthetic-only addition** (PavelDoGreat's 2D curl formulation), never part of the gated path (§ 3.3).
- **Precision:** **f32 for all gated state** (velocity, pressure, sim-res density). f16 would break the run-twice / determinism claim; f16 is permitted ONLY for the non-gated high-res dye buffer (§ 3.3). Determinism posture: browser `epsilon` (like rd2d), reference `bit-exact-same-hw`.

### 3.2 Exploratory accuracy toggles (OFF during gate; the A/B payload)
- **MacCormack limiter** (Selle 2008 revert-to-first-order — the authors' preferred variant; GPU Gems 30 implements the clamp variant) — zero extra advections; kills overshoot on sharp features. Pairs with the splat/obstacle scenes where overshoot rings are visible.
- **MAC-staggered grid** — face-centered velocity, cell-centered pressure; the single highest-value accuracy upgrade, directly attacking the collocated null-space and its O(dx²) residual. Pairs with the **divergence-residual heatmap** A/B (watch it drop). *Cost: a separate solver variant — v2 headline (§ 5).*
- **IVOCK vorticity restoration** (Zhang 2015) — 10–25% cost, projection RHS untouched → an energy-diagnostic upgrade, not a correctness risk.
- **Advection-reflection** (`R = 2P − I` at the half step, Zehnder 2018) — costed honestly: 2× advections + 2× projections per step; energy-preservation A/B on the § 4.3 plot.
- **Jacobi warm-start + decay** — seed the pressure solve from the previous frame's pressure scaled by a decay factor (PavelDoGreat's exact production trick, `PRESSURE: 0.8`; v0.2 provenance). Live-loop only; the canonical stays zero-init-20. The divergence HUD makes the convergence boost *measurable*, not folklore.
- **Live Jacobi iteration count** (10–120, Harris's 40–80 sweet spot marked) — watch the residual floor stop improving past the point where the collocated inconsistency dominates: the O(dx²) story told by a slider.

### 3.3 Aesthetic layer (never affects gate/determinism/MMS — must be toggleable off during verification)
- **Decoupled dye:** 256²-class sim velocity, high-res (up to 1024²) passive dye advected by sampling velocity bilinearly (PavelDoGreat). **Distinct from the gated sim-res density field** (which is part of the canonical capture, § 3.1); the dye buffer is presentation-only, f16-eligible, and separately non-negativity-sane (mirroring the `smoke_density_nonneg` invariant as a display-side assertion, not a gate).
- **2D vorticity confinement** — flagged **aesthetic and web-only** (not in the 2D reference): non-physical forcing that *worsens* energy conservation, so OFF during energy diagnostics and any canonical run. `CURL: 30`-class default per Pavel.
- **Bloom / sunrays / shading / dithering** — pure post-processing (§ 4.4).

### 3.4 Performance architecture (v0.2 addition — "many effects on screen" is an engineering budget, not a hope)

The demo runs sim (256²) + high-res dye (1024²) + post stack at 60 fps with the PROVE diagnostics live. The budget that makes that true:

- **One `GPUComputePassEncoder` per frame, all bind groups pre-created.** At 128²–256² every kernel is tiny (16k–64k threads); the frame cost is dispatch/barrier overhead, not ALU. ~30 dispatches/step (MacCormack = 2 SL passes + correct, diffuse ×2, divergence, 20× Jacobi, gradient-subtract, density SL, dye SL) is fine *if* recorded in one pass with zero per-dispatch allocation. Fuse trivially-fusable kernels (divergence + curl in one dispatch feeds both the projection and the vorticity/curl view).
- **Workgroups 8×8** (64 invocations — the wave-width default per WebGPU guidance), 16×16 as a measured alternative, never hardcoded assumptions about subgroup size.
- **Storage layout:** ping-pong `r32float` storage textures for scalar fields (pressure, divergence, density) and either `rg32float`-as-buffer or two r32 textures for velocity; the dye lives in filterable `rgba16float` textures for the render path. WGSL cannot read+write the same storage texture binding — ping-pong is the contract, not an optimization.
- **All per-frame diagnostics GPU-resident.** Energy, enstrophy, max|∇·u|, and the live-gate max_abs are computed by a two-pass workgroup reduction (tile-reduce → partials buffer → single-workgroup finish), with `enable subgroups` (`subgroupAdd`/`subgroupMax`, Chrome 134+) as a feature-detected fast path over the barrier-tree fallback. The HUD render pass reads the result buffer directly; CPU readback (async `mapAsync`, never awaited in-frame) happens only every N frames for the numeric text.
- **`shader-f16` is a progressive enhancement only** (Chrome 120+, absent on Qualcomm Adreno): dye/post chain only; pressure and all gated state stay f32 (both for determinism and because f16 stalls Jacobi convergence).
- **Perf HUD via `timestamp-query`** (Chrome 121+): per-pass-group GPU timings, displayed with a "quantized to 100 µs" badge (the default Chrome quantization), negative-delta-guarded, degrading to RAF timing when the feature is absent. This is also the honest stage for the exploratory-toggle *costs* — IVOCK's +10–25% and reflection's ~2× are displayed as measured numbers next to the papers' claims.
- **Jacobi shared-memory batching** (k inner iterations per dispatch in an 8×8+halo `var<workgroup>` tile; arXiv:2006.16465 reports up to ~6× for 2D structured problems) — an *exploratory, measured* optimization: the stale-halo effect slows mathematical convergence at tile edges, so it ships only with a before/after in the perf HUD and stays OFF on the gated path (which must match the reference's plain global-memory Jacobi semantics exactly).
- **Gate-run cost sanity:** the live gate re-run is 1000 steps × ~30 dispatches at 128² — a few seconds on any WebGPU device; run it as a blocking-UI-free scratch job (rd2d's `runCaptureExclusive` pattern).

## 4. Scope — four layers

### 4.1 INTERACT
- **Mouse/touch force + dye splat** — drag injects velocity + dye (Pavel-class feel: force ~6000, radius ~0.25 as starting defaults). Splat radius + strength sliders; multi-touch = multiple simultaneous splats.
- **Scene-template gallery (v0.2 expansion — the "templates for a user" surface).** Data-spine-defined full scenes (IC + params + palette + render mode), not just parameter presets:
  1. **Kelvin–Helmholtz (the gated canonical)** — the actual `lid-driven-cavity` shear-layer IC, honestly labeled ("this exact configuration is what the gate certifies"); watch the roll-up.
  2. **Taylor–Green vortex (2D)** — the analytic-solution scene; drives the § 4.3 decay overlay.
  3. **Vortex-pair collision** — two counter-rotating splats.
  4. **Buoyant plume** ("smoke") — bottom heat/dye source, upward body force, low dissipation.
  5. **Ink drop** ("ink-in-water") — high dye persistence, near-zero forcing.
