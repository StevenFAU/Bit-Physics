## 1. Purpose

Bring your prototype-quality 2D flocking to the site as a new sim, and take a niche the § 2.2 survey shows is **uncontested**: a WebGPU boids demo that is *simultaneously* (1) **10⁵–10⁶ agents** via a counting-sort spatial-hash grid, (2) **measuring** the order parameters φ/m/ξ live, (3) reproducing the **Vicsek η→φ order–disorder transition** as a verifiable curve, (4) **proving brute-force ≡ sorted-grid bit-identically** via order-independent i32 fixed-point neighbour accumulation, and (5) letting the flock enter a **two-way fluid medium** whose exchange/projection/advection properties are verified in the page. This directly answers the founding complaint of this whole effort ("the deployed boids is very poor compared to the html prototype"). The deployed `boids-3d` is a minimal 3D sim; this ships the *rich 2D prototype* as its own sim, with an **exceptional verification-visible story built in**: unlike a typical "experimental" demo, it can prove a real determinism claim (the optimized broadphase is bit-identical to the O(n²) oracle) *and* exhibit a real, measurable physics phase transition from day one.

### 2.2 Prior art & positioning (web survey, 2026-07-03; re-checked 2026-07-05)

**The models & observables (the science):**
- **Reynolds 1987** — separation / alignment / cohesion steering, distance+angle neighbourhood ([red3d.com/cwr/boids](https://www.red3d.com/cwr/boids/); SIGGRAPH '87 21(4):25–34).
- **Vicsek 1995** — self-propelled particles with alignment + angular noise. Kinetic order parameter **vₐ = (1/Nv)|Σvᵢ|**, 0 (gas) → 1 (ordered) — *vₐ is Vicsek's own symbol; later work (incl. this demo's UI) writes it **φ** or **Oₚ***. Update θ(t+1)=⟨θ⟩_r + Δθ with **Δθ uniform on [−η/2, +η/2]** (η = the full noise width), constant speed. Vicsek's original transition was **continuous** (vₐ∼(η_c−η)^β, β≈0.45); **Grégoire & Chaté 2004** (PRL 92:025702) and **Chaté et al. 2008** ([PRE 77:046113](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.77.046113)) showed it is **discontinuous (first-order) with traveling density bands** in the large-system limit — using *vectorial* noise (random vector added to the velocity **sum** pre-normalize) vs Vicsek's *scalar/angular* noise; **the noise type changes the transition order**, so the demo must state which it uses. Native η_c≈2.9 at ρ=0.4 (η_max=2π). **This demo normalizes η so η=1 is the fully-random 2π limit (WGSL: Δθ∈[−ηπ,+ηπ]); Vicsek's η_c≈2.9 then maps to ≈0.46, which the 0–0.6 slider brackets — but the demo *measures* its η_c from the χ peak rather than asserting it** ([Vicsek model, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicsek_model); PRL 75:1226, title "Novel Type of Phase *Transition* in a System of Self-Driven Particles").
- **Couzin 2002** — zones of repulsion / orientation / attraction; widening the orientation zone sweeps **swarm → torus/mill → dynamic-parallel → highly-parallel** ([PDF](https://jmvidal.cse.sc.edu/library/couzin02a.pdf); JTB 218:1–11, "Collective Memory and Spatial Sorting in Animal Groups").
- **Tunstrøm 2013 — the operational regime thresholds** (the demo's auto-label): from **Oₚ** (group polarization = |mean heading|, 0→1) and **O_r** (mean **normalized angular momentum about the group centre of mass**, 0→1): **polarized Oₚ>0.65 & O_r<0.35; milling Oₚ<0.35 & O_r>0.65; swarm both<0.35** (else *transitional*) ([PLoS Comput Biol 9(2):e1002915](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1002915)). **⚠ To use these cutoffs honestly the demo's `m` must BE O_r (normalized angular momentum about the *current* COM). The prototype's `regimeName()` uses hand-tuned thresholds and an `m` measured vs the *lag-1* COM — § 4.3 re-locks both to Tunstrøm.**
- **Ballerini 2008** — starlings interact **topologically** with a fixed number of neighbours (**"six–seven"**, the paper's own phrasing), not a fixed metric distance ([PNAS 105:1232](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0711437105)).
- **Cavagna 2010** — velocity-fluctuation correlation **C(r)** (mean inner product of velocity fluctuations at separation r), correlation length **ξ = zero-crossing of C(r)**; the scale-free result is **ξ = aL with a = 0.35** (linear fit, r=0.98, p<10⁻¹⁶ — an exact, quotable value, not a hand-wave) ([PNAS 107:11865](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1005766107); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2900681/)).
- **Susceptibility** **χ = N(⟨φ²⟩−⟨φ⟩²)** peaking at η_c is **not** in Cavagna 2010 (that paper has no susceptibility — earlier drafts miscited it here). It is the standard **Vicsek finite-size-scaling** diagnostic: **Baglietto, Albano & Candia 2012**, "Criticality and the onset of ordering in the standard Vicsek model" ([Interface Focus 2(6):708](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rsfs.2012.0021)); Baglietto & Albano, PRE 80:050103 (2009).
- **Modern reviews (EXPLAIN anchors):** **Chaté 2020**, "Dry Aligning Dilute Active Matter" (Annu. Rev. Condens. Matter Phys. **11**:189) — the authoritative modern treatment of the flocking transition, traveling bands, and giant density fluctuations; **Marchetti et al. 2013**, "Hydrodynamics of Soft Active Matter" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2929); Rev. Mod. Phys. **85**:1143).

**The fluid / wet-active-matter basis (2026-07-05 second pass):**
- **Stam 1999 Stable Fluids** ([PDF](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~chaol/data/cs777/stam-stable_fluids.pdf); original mirror [ns.pdf](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/public_user/stam/reality/Research/pdf/ns.pdf)) is the correct browser-scale substrate for this demo: semi-Lagrangian self-advection, implicit viscosity, and projection are stable at interactive timesteps and explicitly designed for user-applied forces and swirling real-time flows. The tradeoff is numerical diffusion; the demo must call the projection **divergence-damped**, not exact.
- **Harris, GPU Gems ch.38** ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-vi-beyond-triangles/chapter-38-fast-fluid-dynamics-simulation-gpu)) is the implementation anchor: grid velocity fields map naturally to GPU textures/buffers, and the math-to-GPU explanation is exactly the style this repo's EXPLAIN layer should mirror.
- **Fedkiw, Stam & Jensen 2001** ([Stanford](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/smoke/)) adds the graphics lesson: vorticity confinement can restore small-scale swirl lost to coarse stable-fluid grids, but it is a v2+ enhancement unless measured; v1 should first ship the already-proven v4 V-cycle projection and field overlay.
- **Wet active matter honesty:** Marchetti et al. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2929)) distinguish **dry** active matter (substrate/friction; momentum not conserved) from **wet** suspensions where orientation, flow, and concentration interact; Simha & Ramaswamy 2002 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0108301)) specifically predicts coupled orientation-flow-concentration modes; Saintillan & Shelley 2013 ([PDF](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/physique/item/10.1016/j.crhy.2013.04.001.pdf)) frames swimmer forcing as stresslets/pushers/pullers. **The v4 solver is inspired by this wet line but is not a full active-suspension model**: it implements symmetric drag exchange between agents and a 2D Eulerian velocity field, not Jeffery or stresslet orientation dynamics. Copy should say "two-way active medium" / "wet-inspired flocking," not "bacterial suspension" or "Navier-Stokes-accurate animal flock."
- **Boid-fluid implementations are thin.** A 2023 DirectX/SPH paper ([arXiv:2311.15088](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.15088)) explicitly notes that SPH literature seldom includes virtual boid agents and demonstrates two-way SPH↔boid interactions, but at far lower scale and without browser verification. WebGPU fluid repos exist (e.g. [kishimisu/WebGPU-Fluid-Simulation](https://github.com/kishimisu/WebGPU-Fluid-Simulation), [jeantimex/fluid](https://github.com/jeantimex/fluid), [matsuoka-601/WebGPU-Ocean](https://github.com/matsuoka-601/webgpu-ocean)), but they are fluid demos, not million-agent flocking labs, and they do not ship proof rows. This keeps the combined boids+verified-fluid niche open.

**The optimization (the load-bearing part) — see § 3:** counting-sort spatial hash (Green, "Particle Simulation using CUDA" — the histogram→scan→scatter→cellStart/cellEnd→3×3-gather pipeline, plus his Z-order-hash and data-reorder coherence tips; Le Grand, GPU Gems 3 ch.32; **Hoetzlein, GTC 2014 "Fast Fixed-Radius Nearest Neighbors"** — the counting-sort method jtsorlinis copies), **work-efficient Blelloch scan** (Harris/Sengupta/Owens, GPU Gems 3 ch.39 — O(n) up-sweep/down-sweep; **note jtsorlinis's inner scan is Hillis-Steele, work-*inefficient* O(n log n), so the prototype's ported Blelloch is already the stronger one** — a real edge, not a copy), i32 fixed-point for deterministic accumulation (**WGSL atomics are only i32/u32** — [W3C WGSL § 6.2.8](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/#atomic-types); [gpuweb #4894](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/issues/4894) requests f32 atomics; [Toji fixed-point](https://toji.dev/webgpu-best-practices/compute-vertex-data.html); [NVIDIA CCCL FP-determinism](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/controlling-floating-point-determinism-in-nvidia-cccl/) — "a fixed, hierarchical tree rather than … atomics"), and WebGPU many-agent perf (official [computeBoids](https://webgpu.github.io/webgpu-samples/samples/computeBoids/) is O(n²), ~1.5k agents, brute caps ~50k even on GPU; [jtsorlinis/BoidsWebGPU](https://github.com/jtsorlinis/BoidsWebGPU) **self-reports ~16M 2D / ~4M 3D at 60 fps on an M1 Max** — an uninstrumented single-author ceiling, cite as order-of-magnitude, **not** a reproducible benchmark).

**White space (positioning).** (a) **Scale × science together** — large-scale demos (BoidsWebGPU) measure no physics; science visualizations don't hit 10⁶ in-browser; none pair 10⁶ agents with live φ/m/ξ. (b) **The Vicsek η→φ transition** is standard in the literature but **absent from interactive web boids**. (c) **No web demo proves brute ≡ sorted-grid** — the i32 fixed-point trick is documented for WebGPU but not used as a boids verification feature. (d) **Inline science** (ξ≈0.35L, auto-labeled Couzin/PLoS regimes) is absent. (e) **Template-as-evidence UX** is absent: demos expose knobs, not shareable, cited, expected-observable templates with pass/fail posture. (f) **Verified flock-fluid coupling is open ground**: fluid demos have no flocking science or proof rows, and boids demos either use static/vector flow fields or no fluid at all. The defensible novelty is the **intersection** — scale, measured active matter, path equivalence, and a wet-inspired coupled medium — and the prototype occupies it.

## 3. Optimization architecture (the priority)

The whole point is *many agents on screen*. The prototype's pipeline (port verbatim; cross-referenced to the GPU canon):

### 3.1 Counting-sort spatial-hash broadphase (10⁶ agents)

Uniform grid, cell size ≥ perception radius so neighbours live in the wrapped 3×3 stencil. Six GPU stages (prototype ~lines 2049–2231, dispatched ~3145–3154):
1. **clearCounts** — zero per-cell histogram.
2. **histogram** — each agent atomically increments its cell count.
3. **scan** — exclusive prefix-sum of counts → per-cell start offsets, via a **two-level work-efficient Blelloch scan** (per-block scan → scan block-sums → add block offsets). (Harris/Sengupta, GPU Gems 3 ch.39.)
4. **seedCursor** — copy counts to a scatter cursor.
5. **scatter** — write each agent's index into its cell slot → `sortedIdx` (cell-sorted).
6. **simSort** — each agent walks `[cellStart[c], cellCount[c])` over the 3×3 wrapped cells; **no per-cell cap, nothing dropped — the neighbour set is exact**.

Grid capacity-clamped ≤ 262,144 cells (cells may exceed the perception radius at extreme N — the 3×3 stencil stays correct, only slower). Refs: Green "Particle Simulation using CUDA" ([PDF](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/assets/cuda/files/particles.pdf)); Le Grand, GPU Gems 3 ch.32 ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-v-physics-simulation/chapter-32-broad-phase-collision-detection-cuda)).

### 3.2 Fixed-point bit-exactness (determinism = a verification asset)

**Why:** float addition is non-associative, so any order-varying float sum is non-reproducible; and **WGSL has no float atomics** ([Tour of WGSL](https://google.github.io/tour-of-wgsl/types/atomics/atomic-types/); [gpuweb #4894](https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/issues/4894) is still open). **The mechanism (stated precisely — the spec's earlier "atomicAdd on fixed-point contributions" was imprecise):** the neighbour sums are **per-agent local i32 accumulators** (`sepXi/aliXi/cohXi`, each contribution `round(x·FP)` with **FP = 2²⁰**), *not* atomics — the only atomics in the pipeline are the u32 cell histogram/scatter and the saturation flag. Because integer addition is associative **and commutative**, each agent's accumulated sum is **identical regardless of the order it visits its neighbours** — and brute (walks `j=0..n`) and sort (walks the 3×3 cell block) visit the *same* neighbour set. Hence **brute O(n²) ≡ sorted-grid, bit-identical** on the single-species metric path (prototype ~line 1945). This is subtler and stronger than "deterministic atomic reduction": it needs no atomics at all on the hot path.

**Range / saturation:** a fixed 2²⁰ scale in a 32-bit int leaves ~±2¹¹ integer headroom; dense pile-ups can overflow. The prototype's live **saturation detector** (satM > 2³⁰ **or** nSep > 2000 → `satHit()`, prototype ~line 1961) downgrades the run's posture to `epsilon` **honestly**. **Roadmap upgrade (§ 3.6): emulate i64 fixed-point as two u32 (hi/lo) on saturation so the run stays *bit-exact* instead of dropping to epsilon** — a genuine robustness win the prototype leaves on the table. Multi-species / topological keep float accumulation (`epsilon` by construction). Refs: [Toji fixed-point](https://toji.dev/webgpu-best-practices/compute-vertex-data.html) (the `QUANTIZE_FACTOR` pattern); [NVIDIA CCCL FP-determinism](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/controlling-floating-point-determinism-in-nvidia-cccl/).

### 3.3 Exact integer GPU reduction for order parameters

φ (polarization) and m (rotation) come from a per-workgroup i32-partial reduction (fixed-point constants RED_FPV=2²⁰ etc., prototype ~line 825), giving float64-exact sums below 2⁵³ — so the order parameters are exact and **scale to 10⁶** without a CPU readback of every agent.

### 3.4 Rendering & scaling at 10⁶

Instanced point sprites, **additive blending (order-independent, no depth sort)**; **density-preserving world scale √(N/4000)** keeps neighbourhoods — hence the physics — invariant as N grows; point size floors ~1.5 px and glow normalizes by √(4000/N) at 10⁶ so the image neither vanishes nor blooms (prototype ~line 392). Refs: [Codrops WebGPU rendering](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/26/webgpu-fluid-simulations-high-performance-real-time-rendering/).

### 3.5 Perf targets & budget

- **Headline (stated with hardware honesty, not a single number):** target **1,000,000 agents at interactive fps on a mid-range discrete GPU**; jtsorlinis's ~16M-2D figure is a self-reported M1-Max ceiling (§ 2.2), so 10⁶ is a *conservative, defensible* target, not a stretch. **Publish a measured fps-vs-N-vs-adapter table from the capture harness** rather than one hero number — the same honesty posture as the physics.
- **Adaptive tiering (must specify a floor experience):** detect the GPU tier and cap N accordingly — e.g. dGPU → 10⁶, integrated → 10⁵–2.5·10⁵, mobile/software (lavapipe) → ≤ 6.4·10⁴. The default N on load should be the tier's *comfortable* count, not its max, so first paint is smooth everywhere.
- **Brute O(n²):** the exact **verification oracle**, allowed < 16k; N ≥ 16k forces sort (prototype `bruteLocked`, ~line 921). CPU fallback capped 2,000.
- **Memory & WebGPU limits (all verified against defaults):** ~44 B/agent → 1M ≈ ~44 MB, well under `maxStorageBufferBindingSize` (128 MiB) and `maxBufferSize` (256 MiB). Dispatch `ceil(N/256)` workgroups (3,907 at 1M — far below `maxComputeWorkgroupsPerDimension` = 65,535); **never one workgroup per agent.** The real squeeze is **`maxStorageBuffersPerShaderStage` = 8**, which the prototype's `WGSL_SORT` already hits *exactly* (inB, outB, cellCount, cellStart, blockSums, sortedIdx, vortOut, specIn = 8) — any new buffer forces SoA packing or scratch reuse. Note this constraint in the port so it isn't rediscovered by a device-loss.
- **Effect budget at 10⁶:** every always-on visual effect must be **O(screen)** or **O(N) with one simple vertex/fragment op**. Allowed by default: feedback trails, additive sprites, scalar colormap, velocity stretch, HUD reductions, and the φ×m diagram. The coupled-fluid solver is v1 scope but **tier-gated and opt-in by template/slider**: at `coupleStr=0` it allocates no fluid buffers and dispatches no fluid work; above zero it reports its own `fluid_ms`, grid resolution, residual, and saturation posture. Disabled above the tier cap unless explicitly requested: full per-agent readback, rewind checkpoints, g(r)/NND/C(r) lab plots, topological k-NN, and multi-species beyond predator-prey. The acceptance table must report `sim_ms`, `fluid_ms`, and `render_ms`; "many effects on screen" is not allowed to hide a stalled solver.

### 3.6 Optimization roadmap (verified additions beyond the port)

The port ships the prototype's pipeline verbatim (§ 3.1–3.4); these are researched, source-verified upgrades to stage in as perf/robustness headroom (none block v1):

1. **i64 fixed-point on saturation (bit-exact, not epsilon).** Emulate 64-bit fixed point as two u32 (hi/lo carry) when the i32 accumulator would saturate, keeping the run on the **bit-exact** posture instead of downgrading (§ 3.2). The highest-value upgrade because it protects the moat's headline claim at extreme density.
2. **Morton / Z-order cell hashing** instead of linear `y·cols+x`. Interleaving coordinate bits keeps spatially-near cells numerically-near → coalesced neighbour gathers; ~1.5–4× locality wins reported in particle/voxel work, and Green explicitly suggests it. Cheap; preserves exactness (only the cell-index function changes).
3. **Reorder boid data into cell-sorted order** before the force pass (Green's "additional optimization") so the 3×3 read is coalesced rather than gathered through `sortedIdx` indirection. **Tradeoff:** it complicates the `outB[i]`-by-original-index identity that the brute≡sort SHA relies on, so it needs a permutation-aware hash — stage only if profiling shows the gather is the bottleneck.
4. **Work-efficient scan is already ours.** The ported Blelloch up-sweep/down-sweep is O(n); jtsorlinis's Hillis-Steele is O(n log n) (§ 2.2). Keep and advertise this — it's a real edge over the most-cited large-scale web boids demo.
5. **Render at scale:** instanced additive point sprites via **storage-buffer vertex pulling** + **`drawIndirect`** (instance count written by a compute pass, no CPU readback); or jtsorlinis's compute-rasterized point buffer as an alternative. Additive blending is order-independent → no depth sort (§ 3.4).
6. **Per-workgroup shared-memory partials** already used in the exact integer reduction (§ 3.3) — the same "hierarchical tree, not atomics" structure NVIDIA recommends for determinism; keep it as the template if any new reduction is added.

### 3.7 Two-way fluid coupling (v1c, after dry proof)

The v4 prototype's two-way stable-fluid coupling is no longer speculative: `boids-v4-FLUID-NOTES.md` records a measured mirror, GPU verification rows, cpl=0 byte-equality to v3, checkpointed field state, and coupled brute≡sort hashes on RADV. Include it in the final v1, but stage it as **v1c** after v1a/v1b retire the dry-boids proof and UI risks.

**Algorithm to port from v4, not redesign:**
- **Separate modules / bind groups:** `WGSL_FLUID` + `WGSL_COUPLE` stay separate from boid sim bind groups, preserving the sort path's 8-storage-buffer budget. Fluid buffers are lazy: no buffer allocation and no dispatch while `coupleStr=0`.
- **Canonical order:** `sim pass → fluid block → order reduction`. The reduction sees post-coupling velocities when fluid is active; at `coupleStr=0`, the v4 gate proves byte-equality to the fluidless canonical rather than assuming it.
- **Exchange:** sample the cell-centered periodic velocity field bilinearly, drag each agent toward local `u`, and splat the equal-and-opposite momentum into a per-cell i32 fixed-point accumulator (`FLUID_SCALE=2²⁰`). The agent receives the quantized impulse actually deposited, so the exchange-conservation row has teeth.
- **Solver:** `clearAcc → couple → stir → applyForce → advect → diffuse → project`, with semi-Lagrangian advection, implicit Jacobi viscosity, and a 2-level damped-Jacobi V-cycle pressure projection. The projection is **divergence-damped**, not exact; the live residual is displayed.
- **Grid tiers:** `fluidGridH ∈ {64,96,128,192}` with aspect-tracked columns rounded to multiples of 4; default 96. Grid size is independent of agent count, which is why this is the right fluid pairing for 10⁵–10⁶ boids. Publish the measured `fluid_ms` vs grid table.
- **Cursor stir + field viz:** herd/lure pointer motion deposits a bounded Gaussian impulse; field overlay is Off/Speed/Curl with alpha cap so it explains the medium without hiding agents.
- **State/capture:** checkpoints carry `{fgw,fgh,fdata}` when coupling is active, and restore either writes the field back or zeroes it with a note. Share URLs include `coupleStr`, `fluidVisc`, `fluidGridH`, and `fluidShow`.

**Proof rows to port, as a separate block:** coupling gate (`cpl=0` before/after fluid activity), exchange momentum conservation, projection residual, MMS Laplacian order ≈2, advection stability, coupled hash repeatability, and coupled brute≡sort at N=4096·60 steps. These rows match the repo's other fluid sims: eulerian-smoke treats stable-fluids projection as a measured residual, schrodinger-smoke separates exact spectral identities from approximate circulation, and sph-water separates gated primitives from beyond-reference live solver.

**Claim boundary:** this is a **wet-inspired active-medium coupling**, not a full active-suspension solver. It does not implement stresslets, pusher/puller classes, Jeffery rotation, no-slip swimmer bodies, dye transport, vorticity confinement, or SPH/PIC/FLIP free surfaces. Those are v2+ only if a later spec gives them their own proof rows.

**Do not mingle claims:** boid bit-exactness is neighbour-search/path equivalence; fluid correctness is exchange/projection/advection verification; coupled correctness is the combined hash row. They should sit adjacent in PROVE, but each with its own evidence ladder and failure modes.

## 4. Scope — four layers

### 4.1 INTERACT

**Design principle (from the survey): legible by default, deep on demand.** The best-loved boids demos (eater.net's four knobs) win on *legibility*, not knob-count. Default view exposes **~4 headline controls** — noise η, alignment, cohesion, agent-count — each mapping 1:1 to felt behaviour; the full parameter set lives behind a disclosure. Ship **named, defensible default values** (Van Hunter Adams' principle — every default is a value someone can defend), and a "reset to canonical" that restores them.

- **Agent-count selector** 1k → 10⁶ (prototype `cnt`, ~line 376) with the density-preserving scale auto-applied ≥ 32k; default N = the detected tier's *comfortable* count (§ 3.5).
- **Reynolds sliders:** separation / alignment / cohesion / perception range / cruise speed (prototype `S.wSep…maxSpeed`, ~line 489).
- **Vicsek noise η slider** — the phase-transition control (prototype `noise` 0–0.6, ~line 231); dragging it collapses φ through the *measured* η_c live. Label the convention (η=1 ⇒ 2π limit, § 2.2).
- **Field-of-view** (rear blind spot); **broadphase toggle** (brute exact-oracle / sort 10⁶).
- **Cursor tools (delight is here — survey topic 3):** **Predator** (repel; the escape steer *overrides* alignment/cohesion so the flock visibly *tears open then heals* — the money shot) and **Attractor / bait** (a glowing point that draws the flock into natural vortices, à la Hodgin's *Murmuration*), plus the prototype's **herd / lure / place-obstacle**. A global **Wind** slider (one vector force reshapes the whole flock).
- **Fluid controls (v1c, advanced but visible):** `Coupling`, `Viscosity`, `Fluid grid`, and `Field: Off | Speed | Curl`, plus a **Fluid drift** template that turns coupling on with measured-safe defaults. The main UI should make the dry/wet toggle legible: `coupleStr=0` means no fluid work and the dry proof applies; `coupleStr>0` means the fluid proof block becomes active and the order parameters are measured post-coupling.
- **Templates as first-class, shareable artifacts (survey topic 4):**
  - **Preset gallery with mini-loop thumbnails** (data-spine defined, § 5): ordered flock, gas/disorder, mill/torus, swarm, dynamic-parallel, murmuration, **predator-panic**, traveling bands (near η_c, high v), the **Vicsek noise-sweep**, the **brute≡grid verification** template, **Fluid drift**, **stirred current**, and **curl river**. Each captioned with its § 2.2 source and one-line physics.
  - **Shareable URL encodes the full parameter state + seed** (prototype `buildShareURL`, `STATE_KEYS`) — because the run is deterministic, *a link reproduces the bit-identical trajectory on the same device.* The determinism claim becomes literally shareable; make this a headline, not a footnote.
  - **Dice / randomize** (seeded) and an **A/B morph** that lerps between two presets so users explore the space by feel.
  - **The clickable parameter-space map (flagship "explore" panel).** Promote the headless **η×ρ noise-sweep heatmap** (already built, colored by φ) from a static readout to an **interactive 2-D regime map**: click a point in (noise, density) — or a (noise, orientation-zone) Couzin map — and the live flock **jumps to that regime**, with the named phase (swarm / torus / parallel / gas) shown. This is scientifically honest (it *is* the Couzin/Vicsek phase diagram) and, per the survey, genuinely novel as boids UX.
  - **Template contract, not just UI state:** every template emitted by the data spine has `id`, `label`, `seed`, `params`, `expected_observables` (ranges for φ, O_r, ξ or χ where applicable), `posture` (`bit-exact`, `epsilon`, or `visual-only`), `citation_url`, `caption`, `thumbnail_frame`, and `loop_frame_range`. This lets templates power the UI, poster generation, PROVE expectations, and link sharing from one source of truth.
- **Rewind / scrub (determinism as a gameplay feature — promote to v1).** The deterministic checkpoint ring (prototype, ≤200k) lets a user rewind, poke the flock differently, and watch a new branch — the single most *on-thesis* interaction, since it dramatizes the determinism the PROVE layer certifies. See § 5.
- All controls live-loop; a pinned canonical seed drives capture (Step B).

### 4.2 EXPLAIN

- **The models:** Reynolds' three rules, the Vicsek update (`θ(t+1) ← ⟨θ⟩_r + Δθ`, `Δθ` uniform on `[−η/2, +η/2]`, constant-speed advance — with the demo's normalized-η convention stated, § 2.2), and the order-parameter definitions (φ=Oₚ, m=O_r, ξ, χ) — each linked to the ported WGSL rule block (the prototype defines the rule ONCE and interpolates it into both `main` and `simSort`, ~line 1895). Citations from § 2.2. Flag the *angular-vs-vectorial noise* choice, since it sets the transition order.
- **The optimization, inline:** *why* counting sort suits a fixed grid (histogram→scan→scatter is one stable O(n) pass), and *why* i32 fixed-point makes brute ≡ sort bit-exact (integer add is associative; WGSL has no float atomics). This is optimization pedagogy no boids demo offers.
- **The fluid, inline:** a small pipeline diagram `agents → splat exchange → advect → diffuse → project → post-coupling φ/m`, with three honesty labels: semi-Lagrangian advection is stable but diffusive; projection is divergence-damped with a measured residual; exchange conservation is a row the page computes, not prose. The wet-active-matter panel should explicitly say this is a drag-coupled active medium, not a full pusher/puller suspension (§ 2.2 / § 3.7).
- Hand-rolled markup on `common/common-web/src/theme.css`; snippet anchors extracted at build time (§ 5).

### 4.3 PROVE (exceptional — a real verification story from day one)

- **Flagship — brute ≡ sort bit-exactness, with a crisp evidence ladder (don't blur the scales):**
  - **At N=4096:** run the O(n²) oracle and the sorted-grid path from the same seed, SHA-256 both trajectories (60 steps), show **bit-identical** (prototype's in-page proof, ~line 1490). The optimized broadphase *provably* computes the same physics as the reference — a claim no surveyed web boids demo makes.
  - **At 10⁶:** brute is locked (< 16k only), so the bit-exact evidence there is **(a) the φ-series hash run-twice repeatable** (`hashOrderRun`, scalable, runs even at 10⁶) **and (b) sort≡sort trajectory-hash repeatability** — *not* brute≡sort. State this ladder explicitly so "bit-exact at a million agents" is never overclaimed.
- **The Vicsek transition, measured:** the noise-sweep ramps η and plots **φ(η) collapsing through η_c**, with the **χ = N·Var(φ) peak** (the Baglietto/Albano finite-size-scaling diagnostic, § 2.2 — *not* Cavagna) locating the transition (prototype noise sweep, ~line 428). A verifiable order–disorder phase transition on the honesty surface; the demo *measures* η_c rather than asserting a literature value.
- **Live order parameters + auto-regime label — re-locked to Tunstrøm (honesty fix):** φ (=Oₚ), **m defined as O_r = mean normalized angular momentum about the *current* COM**, and ξ (from C(r)) via the exact integer reduction, with the **confirmed Tunstrøm 2013 thresholds** auto-labeling the live state — polarized (Oₚ>0.65 & O_r<0.35) / milling (Oₚ<0.35 & O_r>0.65) / swarm (both<0.35) / transitional. **⚠ Port note:** the prototype's `regimeName()` (~line 846) uses *hand-tuned* cutoffs and an `m` measured vs the **lag-1** COM — both must be replaced with Tunstrøm's definitions and numbers, or the "the sim tells you its regime, from measured numbers" claim doesn't survive contact with the cited paper. If a heuristic is kept for aesthetics, label it "demo heuristic," not "PLoS."
- **Determinism posture, stated honestly (terminology cleaned up):** "backend" is overloaded in this repo — reserve it for Stacks B/C/D and say **path** for brute vs sort. The claim is: same-seed + same-device single-species-metric runs are **bit-identical between the brute path and the sort path** (i32 fixed-point neighbour sums; proven in-page by the trajectory SHA-256). **Cross-*device* bit-exactness is NOT claimed** (vendors round sin/cos/normalize differently and flocking is chaotic) → cross-device correctness lives in the **φ/m distributions** (prototype ~line 452), and cross-*Stack* (B↔D) equivalence is likewise distributional, never pointwise. η>0 stays deterministic (per-agent hash of seed⊕step; note CPU and GPU use *independent* hash constants, so the CPU fallback is bit-exact vs itself but **not comparable to the GPU**). The fp-saturation / multi-species / topological `epsilon` downgrades are shown, not hidden.
- **In-page verification suite** (port, prototype ~line 1363): 11 headless reference-rule checks (determinism, permutation symmetry, toroidal translation, speed/box/finite invariants, grid≡brute single-step neighbours, FOV-responsive, alignment↑⇒φ↑, noise↑⇒φ↓, topological well-defined, susceptibility finite, NND/g(r) finite) + a GPU device-scoped section (scan ≡ CPU integer scan; sortedIdx a valid cell-consistent permutation; occupancy = no drops by construction; fp-saturation; φ-series hash repeatable at current N; trajectory-hash repeatable; brute ≡ sort SHA). Rendered as PASS/FAIL/SOFT/SKIP rows — the demo runs its own tests in front of the user.
- **Fluid proof block (v1c, separate evidence ladder):** port the v4 rows after the dry rows: `fluid · coupling gate`, `fluid · exchange conserves momentum`, `fluid · projection residual`, `fluid · MMS Laplacian order ≈ 2`, `fluid · advection stable`, `fluid · coupled hash repeatable`, and `fluid · brute ≡ sort (coupled)`. A green coupled row means the two-way system is path-equivalent at N=4096·60 on the current adapter; it does **not** imply cross-device pointwise replay or a full biological active-suspension model.
- **Falsifiability probe:** force fixed-point saturation (or point at a deliberately wrong reference) → the posture downgrades to `epsilon` / the brute≡sort SHA differs → shown in red. PASS reads as a test result, not decoration.

### 4.4 RENDER — art direction (the "visually stunning" brief)

The survey's core finding: a flock reads as an **organic murmuration** rather than a cheap particle spray when it has three things — **density silhouette** (the flock is a *shape* with thick and wispy regions), **coherent local motion** (neighbours share colour/heading), and **curl/vorticity** in the flow (it swirls, doesn't rigidly translate). The physics already produces all three; the render must expose them.

- **Trails via a full-screen feedback/persistence buffer — the single most scalable beauty technique.** Draw the flock into an offscreen texture, then each frame redraw that texture at ~0.92 alpha before the new frame. Cost is O(screen pixels), **independent of agent count** — 1M agents trail as cheaply as 1k. The prototype's "trail persistence" slider is exactly this; keep it. Ordered flocks leave luminous ribbons, a disordered gas leaves none → **the trails themselves encode the order parameter**, making the phase transition self-evident.
- **Additive soft round sprites, size ∝ local density.** Smoothstep alpha falloff (never hard-edged quads); additive blending is order-independent so density becomes brightness *for free* — the glow is the silhouette, no separate bloom pass needed. Add **size ∝ local density** (the prototype colours by density but sizes uniformly — a cheap, high-impact upgrade) and a mild **velocity stretch** so fast agents smear into motion streaks. Keep the prototype's 10⁶ safeguards: size floor ~1.5 px, glow normalized by √(4000/N).
- **Colour, using the shared module where it fits.** For **heading**, use the repo's existing `cyclic` map in `common/common-web/src/colormap.ts` (added for curl-noise angle data) or the prototype's HSV hue — either is cyclic, so 359°→0° has no false seam. For **scalar fields** (speed / density / fluctuation / curl magnitude / |v−v̄|), adopt `common/common-web/src/colormap.ts` (viridis / turbo / magma / cividis / house aurora/ember via `emitColormapWgsl`) instead of the prototype's hand-rolled 4-stop ramp — better perceptual uniformity *and* portfolio consistency. Keep the prototype's 7 colour modes (heading / alignment / speed / density / fluctuation / curl / species) — "fluctuation" (|v−v̄|) is literally the ξ field the science panel measures.
- **"Alive" for near-free:** a **wing-flap / pulse driven by speed** in the vertex stage, and the velocity stretch above, read as life without touching the sim. Optional; cheap.
- **The φ×m phase-space diagram is a hero visual, not a stat.** The live traveling dot over the (polarization, rotation) plane, with the Tunstrøm regions shaded and labeled, is the most legible way to *see* the flock's state move — render it large and beautiful, not as a thumbnail.
- **Fluid field overlay as a science visual, not a fog layer.** Speed mode shows the invisible medium the flock rides; Curl mode reveals vortices and shear layers. Keep v4's alpha cap and draw it behind/under the particles so it supports the murmuration silhouette. The **Fluid drift** and **stirred current** loop thumbnails should visibly differ from dry murmuration by field overlay plus altered φ×m trace.
- **Palette / type (carry the prototype's identity):** near-black ink background (#04060d), amber (#ffb24a) + teal (#46e0d0) accents, Archivo display + IBM Plex Mono, glassy blurred panels. This is already a strong, coherent dark-mode aesthetic — keep it.
- **Poster + motion loop** (`make-posters.mjs` / `make-loops.mjs`) for the landing card and social. Skip a heavy bloom post by default — additive-density glow carries it at 1M.
- **Visual verification, not taste-only:** add a Playwright screenshot pass for desktop and mobile that asserts the canvas is nonblank, the flock occupies a sane content bbox, controls do not overlap, and each headline template has a distinct thumbnail by cheap image metrics (brightness/content bbox/SSIM threshold). This repo already treats presentation artifacts as testable; the "visually stunning" bar should be captured, not left to memory.

## 5. Port plan — v1 scope vs deferred

**Re-tiering rationale (v0.4).** The prototype *already implements* almost everything, including fluid, so the real axis isn't "build or defer" — it's **(visual-wow × already-built × on-thesis × proof-surface quality) ÷ (port-cost × risk)**. The v0.3 cut treated fluid as a v2 hero expansion because it is a second solver. This pass reverses that: the v4 fluid work has enough proof machinery to include in final v1, as long as it is staged after the dry boids proof and kept under its own rows.

**v1a — core (ship first; retires the perf + moat risk):** 2D Reynolds + Vicsek; counting-sort broadphase to 10⁶; i32 fixed-point bit-exactness (brute≡sort); exact integer φ/m reduction with **m = Tunstrøm O_r** and the confirmed regime thresholds; the core regime presets + Vicsek noise-sweep; the brute≡sort + φ-hash verification suite; the feedback-buffer/additive render (§ 4.4); shared colormaps including the existing cyclic map for angular fields; the four layers; the data spine. The § 3.6-#1 i64-on-saturation upgrade is **v1a-preferred but not gate-blocking** if the saturation detector and posture downgrade are clear; do not risk the port on a new wide-integer path before the core proof lands. **Suggested checkpoint after v1a (see § 6).**

**v1b — delight (cheap because already built; do *not* defer these — they are the brief):**
- **Predator-prey template** (multi-species, epsilon-tagged) + the **Predator/Attractor cursor tools** (§ 4.1) — the single biggest source of "fun" in every surveyed demo. Ships behind the honest `epsilon` posture the prototype already computes.
- **External flow fields** (shear / vortex / double-gyre / source-sink / curl-noise) — visually rich *and* a science tie-in (double-gyre and curl-noise are analytically incompressible, ∇·u=0; source-sink is not — a teachable contrast). Cheap: pure per-agent field math, already ported to CPU+WGSL.
- **Rewind / checkpoint replay** — the most on-thesis interaction (dramatizes the certified determinism), ≤200k agents.
- **The φ×m phase-space diagram (hero visual) + the clickable η×ρ / Couzin parameter-space map** (§ 4.1, § 4.4) — the flagship "explore" surface; the sweep heatmap that powers it is already built.
- **Shareable-URL + preset gallery + dice + A/B morph** (§ 4.1).

**v1c — coupled fluid (hard, but now in scope):** the full **two-way stable-fluids coupling** (Stam advect→diffuse→project + V-cycle projection + fixed-point splat) with the v4 D-F verification rows (§ 3.7). This lands after v1a/v1b because it adds a second proof system, not because it is optional. Required defaults: `coupleStr=0` is byte-inert; the **Fluid drift** template opts in; weak tiers can disable fluid automatically with an explicit posture note and still pass dry v1a/v1b.

**v2+ — deferred features (lower value, epsilon-heavy, or needing their own proof):** **topological k-NN** (epsilon, N≤8k); **multi-species beyond predator-prey** (segregate / mixed / cycle-RPS); the **full measurement lab** (g(r)/NND histograms + C(r) plot beyond the headline ξ); **spatial (hot/cold) noise fields**; **camera zoom/pan**; **fluid dye advection**, **vorticity confinement**, **3-level multigrid**, **stresslet/pusher/puller active-suspension modes**, and **SPH/PIC/FLIP free-surface coupling**. Each must earn a proof row before promotion.

**Data spine:** `packages/boids-2d/web/gen-verification.mjs` — committed generated module (prebuild/predev + idempotence), emitting the regime/template definitions (params + science caption + citation URL + expected observable ranges + posture + thumbnail/loop frames), the WGSL rule/broadphase/fluid snippet anchors, the fluid measured bounds (mirror table + row thresholds), and (Step B) the canonical provenance. Consumes the § 2.2 survey for captions. HARD-FAIL if any template citation, snippet anchor, colormap key, expected-observable field, or fluid bound is missing; the goal is one source of truth for UI, EXPLAIN, PROVE, posters, and share links.

## 7. Governance & constraints

## 9. Out of scope / deferred / rejected-with-cause

- **v2+ deferred (§ 5):** topological k-NN; multi-species beyond predator-prey (segregate / mixed / cycle); the full g(r)/NND/C(r) measurement lab beyond headline ξ; spatial hot/cold noise fields; camera zoom/pan; fluid dye advection; vorticity confinement; 3-level multigrid; stresslet/pusher/puller active-suspension modes; SPH/PIC/FLIP free-surface coupling. *(Note: predator-prey, flow fields, rewind, the clickable parameter-space map, and two-way stable-fluid coupling moved UP into v1.)*
- **Full wet active-suspension claim — REJECTED for v1.** The shipped fluid is drag-coupled stable fluids, not a Saintillan/Shelley stresslet solver or Simha/Ramaswamy hydrodynamic continuum.
