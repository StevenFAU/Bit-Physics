## 1. Purpose

Elevate the Mandelbulb demo from a *camera-orbit-only canvas* into an *instrument* — and reframe the Mandelbulb from decorative fractal art into a **verified closed-form simulation**. The § 2.2 survey is unambiguous: every browser Mandelbulb is visuals-only — e.g. the closest WebGPU reference, [zordone/fractal-webgpu](https://github.com/zordone/fractal-webgpu), whose README (fetched 2026-07-03) describes ray-marched SDF rendering with shading/AO and an animated power parameter, and contains no mathematical explanation, precision statement, or determinism claim of any kind (*this absence is the survey's own gap analysis, not a README quote*). This demo takes the uncontested ground: run-twice bit-identity, a live DE correctness check against **three exact closed-form anchors**, the distance-estimator math inline with the WGSL executing on screen, and a template gallery — while being the **most visually stunning** demo in the portfolio.

Three properties make it uniquely strong:
- **The cleanest f32-vs-f64 story in the portfolio.** The DE is single-pass closed-form (no time-stepping, no accumulation), so its f32-vs-f64 divergence — measured 1.5e-5, matching wgpu-native (`docs/perf-ledger.md:85`) — *is* the single-precision floor, with nothing else to blame. "This is exactly what single precision costs," shown live.
- **Three analytically-derived DE anchors.** `DE(0,0,0) = 0` (inside the set), `DE(10,0,0) = 5·ln 10 = 11.512925464970229` (**exact for the committed kernel**, not merely asymptotic — the escape check precedes the first derivative update, so the point returns with `dz = 1`; the SymPy generator confirms, § 2 FACT), and `DE(1,0,0) = ½·√257·ln√257/(576√2+1) = 0.027268230020419913` (hand-derived surd; escape at the third radius check with `|z| = √257`) — all closed-form, independent of any iteration loop. Authoritative values live in the committed golden table `tools/testkit/golden/tables/closed-form/mandelbulb-de-samples.json` (`expected.DE` fields); derivations in `docs/sim-specs/closed-form/mandelbulb-explorer/algebraic.md:65-143` and `tools/testkit/golden/derivations/mandelbulb-de-samples.md`. The demo evaluates the DE at these points on the visitor's GPU and shows measured-vs-analytic, the way ising plots measured-vs-Yang.
- **The display is fully decoupled from the gate.** The gate reads DE probe points from `mandelbulb_de.wgsl` (compute kernel, pinned p=8 on a 16×16 grid); the *display* is a separate raymarch (`render.wgsl`) the gate never reads. So the entire template gallery + render upgrade is gate-safe (§ 6), and the display-vs-gate distinction is itself an honesty teaching moment (§ 3.2).

### 2.2 Prior art & positioning (web survey, 2026-07-03)

Surveyed the Mandelbulb / distance-estimated-fractal landscape — the formula's origin, the DE + sphere-tracing math, rendering techniques, the software/creative lineage, and browser demos.

- **Formula & origin.** Daniel White & Paul Nylander, 2009: for a point at radius r, polar θ, azimuth φ, the nth power is `vⁿ = rⁿ·(sin nθ cos nφ, sin nθ sin nφ, cos nθ)`, iterate `v → vⁿ + c` ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandelbulb) — "developed in 2009 by Daniel White and Paul Nylander using spherical coordinates"). The name "triplex" for the hypercomplex-style ℝ³ algebra is **Skytopia's/Nylander's term**, not Wikipedia's ([Skytopia p2](https://www.skytopia.com/project/fractal/2mandelbulb.html): "hypercomplex ('triplex') numbers"). **Power 8 is canonical** — Skytopia calls it the "sweet spot for overall detail and beauty"; on *why* higher powers work, White himself: *"I don't know - it's a surprising find to begin with"* ([Skytopia p2](https://www.skytopia.com/project/fractal/2mandelbulb.html)); "The 8th power is traditionally considered the standard Mandelbulb" ([Maths Town all-powers](https://www.maths.town/fractal-articles/mandelbulb/mandelbulb-all-powers/)). Matches this repo's `pow_z` (`packages/mandelbulb-explorer/src/mandelbulb_de.wgsl:12-23`). (In-repo nuance: `docs/sim-specs/closed-form/mandelbulb-explorer/algebraic.md:23` dates the White/Nylander formalization 2007; the published account is 2009 — same Lane-A docs pass as the § 2 upstream flag.)
- **DE + ray marching.** Hart, Sandin & Kauffman 1989, "Ray Tracing Deterministic 3-D Fractals," SIGGRAPH ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/74334.74363)) — the origin of DE-based ray marching of 3D fractals (quaternion Julia sets); the *name* "sphere tracing" is **Hart 1996** (*The Visual Computer* 12(10), DOI 10.1007/s003710050084). Spelling: **Kauffman** (one final n). The practical estimator with running scalar derivative — `dz_{n+1} = p·r^{p-1}·dz_n + 1`, `DE = 0.5·log(r)·r/dz` (≡ the `0.25·log(m)·√m/dz`, m = r², form) — appears verbatim in **Ray Tracing Gems II Ch. 33, "Real-Time Rendering of Complex Fractals" (da Silva, Novello, Lopes & Velho, Apress 2021)** ([chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4842-7185-8_33), [arXiv:2102.01747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.01747), Listing 7) and in [Hvidtfeldt Part V](http://blog.hvidtfeldts.net/index.php/2011/09/distance-estimated-3d-fractals-v-the-mandelbulb-different-de-approximations/) ("DE = 0.5*r*log(r)/dr"). [Quílez's Mandelbulb article](https://iquilezles.org/articles/mandelbulb/) derives the underlying Hubbard–Douady-potential estimate `d = |w|·log|w| / |w′|` (no ½ on the page; his DE *code* lives in the linked Shadertoy [ltfSWn](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/ltfSWn)) — the committed kernel's ½ is the conventional conservative factor of the Hvidtfeldt/RTG-II form. Matches this repo's DE (`packages/mandelbulb-explorer/src/mandelbulb_de.wgsl:25-41`; `docs/sim-specs/closed-form/mandelbulb-explorer/algebraic.md:41-63`).
- **DE rigor (honesty input for § 3.2 — the survey's most load-bearing find).** The scalar running-derivative DE is **heuristic for the Mandelbulb, not a proven bound**: the triplex power is not conformal, so the 2D Hubbard–Douady/Böttcher argument does not carry over — Hvidtfeldt Part V states the Jacobian→scalar argument "is not valid anymore. Despite this limitation, the scalar derivative actually works," and his Part VIII epilogue: the formulas "were never proved for exotic stuff like the Mandelbulb triplex algebra." The demo must therefore be precise about *what is verified*: the golden anchors and the gate verify that **the committed implementation evaluates the declared closed-form estimator exactly** (bit-level, f32-floor-level) — they do not, and cannot, prove the estimator is a true distance lower bound. That distinction is itself an EXPLAIN teaching moment in the repo's voice (§ 3.2).
- **Rendering techniques (the visual toolkit, all gate-safe display-side).** Normal via DE gradient — central differences or tetrahedron ([Quilez normals](https://iquilezles.org/articles/normalsSDF/)); soft shadows via penumbra march `res=min(res,k·h/t)` ([Quilez shadows](https://iquilezles.org/articles/rmshadows/)); ambient occlusion via the standard DE-fractal idioms — sampled-distance taps along the normal and/or orbit-trap proximity — as used in iq's [ltfSWn](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/ltfSWn) and RTG II Ch. 33 (the earlier "multiresaocc" cite was iq's *multiresolution SSAO* article — wrong technique family, dropped); orbit-trap coloring ([Quilez 3D orbit traps](https://iquilezles.org/articles/orbittraps3d/)); smooth (de-banded) iteration count ([Quilez](https://iquilezles.org/articles/msetsmooth/)); Blinn-Phong + glow.
- **Interactive-control taxonomy.** Live uniforms (real-time, no recompile): camera orbit/zoom/pan, light position/softness, **power n (incl. fractional → morph)**, **Julia c-offset (Juliabulb)**, bailout, coloring mode. For Julia mode the display derivative recurrence **drops the `+1`** (`dz_{n+1} = p·r^{p-1}·dz_n`, `dz₀ = 1` — c is constant; [Quilez distance-to-fractals](https://iquilezles.org/articles/distancefractals/), corroborated by RTG II Ch. 33's Julia listing). Structural (avoid): f32↔f64 precision — **WGSL has no f64 type at all** (f32 + gated f16 only, [W3C WGSL spec](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/)) — the deep-zoom limiter ([Ice Fractal](https://icefractal.com/articles/mandelbulb/), [HN precision thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=10956879)).
- **Software / creative lineage.** Mandelbulb 3D ([mandelbulb3d.com](https://mandelbulb3d.com/)); Mandelbulber (Krzysztof Marczak, [mandelbulb.com](https://www.mandelbulb.com/2012/3d-fractal-rendering-mandelbulber/)); Quílez Shadertoys ([ltfSWn](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/ltfSWn), "Mandelbulb - derivative"); Hvidtfeldt's "Distance Estimated 3D Fractals" series; Tom Beddard/subblue — Dec 2009 Mandelbulb ray tracer as a **Pixel Bender / Quartz Composer plugin** (not in-browser; his in-browser Fractal Lab is WebGL, ~2011); WebGL/WebGPU: [wglMandelBulber](https://github.com/BrutPitt/wglMandelBulber), [zordone/fractal-webgpu](https://github.com/zordone/fractal-webgpu).
- **White space (positioning).** (a) **No verification / determinism / precision surfacing** — every browser demo surveyed is visuals-only (gap analysis across READMEs and live demos; the zordone README, fetched in full, contains no math/precision/determinism content — § 1); no demo shows run-twice bit-identity or an f32-vs-f64 DE-probe check. (b) **No re-runnable correctness check.** (c) **Math off-page** — the rigorous DE derivation lives in papers/blogs, never bound to the running shader. (d) **No tie to the DE-bound / complex-dynamics theory — and none states the estimator's heuristic status** (the DE-rigor bullet above); an instrument that is *honest about the math's own limits* is the deepest form of the white space. A *verified* Mandelbulb is genuinely novel and aligns with this portfolio's established instrument pattern.

## 3. Scope

Four additive layers. Nothing here mutates the gate DE kernel `packages/mandelbulb-explorer/src/mandelbulb_de.wgsl`, the capture pinning, the gate, `tolerance*.toml`, or the seed-42 probe grid. The display shader `packages/mandelbulb-explorer/web/src/render.wgsl` **is** in scope: it is presentation, not the gate kernel; the gate reads the DE probe-point buffer via `evalProbeDE` → `exposeCapture` (`tools/productization/web-deploy/verify.py:381-405`; `packages/mandelbulb-explorer/web/src/main.ts:103-141`), never canvas pixels — so render-side changes cannot perturb the gate (§ 6).

### 3.1 Layer 1 — INTERACT (the template gallery + live controls) — the biggest lift

Each is a display-uniform preset.
- **Probe-grid overlay (the gate made spatially visible — unique to this sim).** The 16×16 gate probe grid is *geometry inside the displayed scene*: 256 points on the z=0 plane, box ±1.5, with the seed-42 jitter (`packages/mandelbulb-explorer/web/src/main.ts:38-51`). Render them as an optional overlay in the 3D view — small billboarded markers colored by canonical DE via the shared colormap (206 outside-set / 50 in-set, from the committed canonical) — so the visitor *sees exactly which points the gate verifies, inside the visual they're exploring*. After a § 3.3 live re-run, markers can instead encode the local `|f32 − f64|` residual, hover-linked to the § 3.3 residual heatmap. Display-side geometry only — gate-safe by § 6.
- **Live controls:** power n (continuous, incl. fractional → real-time morph — the classic "unfolding"; clamp to a sane range, e.g. n ∈ [2, 16] — `pow(r, p−1)` misbehaves near r→0 for p < 1); Julia mode + c-offset; iteration count (display fidelity); bailout radius; coloring mode; light direction + softness; camera orbit/zoom/pan (orbit already exists, `:266-293` — add zoom + pan).
- **Honest framing (load-bearing):** every live control changes **only the display**; the **gate always evaluates the committed p=8 kernel on the pinned probe grid**. A persistent "exploring (display) · gated (p=8 probe)" indicator makes this unmissable (§ 3.2 teaching moment); it flips to an "off-canonical" state the moment display params ≠ canonical (p≠8 or Julia mode), with a one-click **"return to verified view"** that restores the canonical-p8 template.

### 3.3 Layer 3 — PROVE (verification bound to real data)

- **Flagship — live DE gate re-run (framed by the § 2 pass-criterion FACT):** evaluate the committed DE kernel at the canonical 16×16 probe grid on the visitor's GPU (the `evalProbeDE` path already exists, `packages/mandelbulb-explorer/web/src/main.ts:103-141`) and compare against the committed **f64 canonical DE values** (extracted from the committed h5 at data-spine time, § 4), mirroring `_gate_mandelbulb` (`tools/productization/web-deploy/verify.py:381-405`). Render **three numbers on one scale** — measured `max_abs` (your GPU), the strict f64 budget `1e-5 × scale ≈ 7.98e-6`, and the recorded f32 floor `1.5e-5` — e.g. as a log-scale number line. The honest caption *is the story*: the browser gate's PASS criterion is run-twice byte-identity (`tools/productization/web-deploy/verify.py:395`); the f32 floor sits ~1.9× **above** the strict f64 budget and the repo **refuses to widen the tolerance** — it reports the miss (`round_trip_at_1e-5: false`) instead of hiding it. No naive `max_abs ≤ budget → PASS/FAIL` chip (§ 2). An instrument that displays the number it *doesn't* clear is the sharpest honesty artifact in the portfolio.
